# PP-101: bounded stochastic population and reaction adapter

Research/proposal checked 28 October 2026 against primary sources and the repository's [PP-101 / issue 56 scope](scientific-backlog.json). No PP-101 code, dependencies, simulations, statistical test batteries or paid calls were run. The recommendation is **Gillespie's direct SSA for two registered, finite-state teaching models**, with explicit count/rate semantics, independently checkable distributions and a separately versioned PRNG. This is not a universal reaction-network or organism-prediction engine.

## Primary algorithm and model class

Daniel T. Gillespie's *Stochastic Simulation of Chemical Kinetics* (2007), DOI `10.1146/annurev.physchem.58.032806.104637`, gives the well-stirred, constant-volume/temperature assumptions in §2, propensity definition (2), unimolecular propensity `a(x)=kx`, and direct SSA equations (10a–b), printed pp. 37–40. Generate `tau=-log(U1)/a0` and select the first cumulative propensity strictly above `U2*a0`; then apply the registered integer stoichiometric change. Its decay example, equations (11)–(14), independently gives the binomial law, mean and standard deviation. “Exact SSA” means exact sampling of the stipulated continuous-time Markov model in ideal arithmetic, not exact physical prediction or immunity to finite PRNG/logarithm error. [Publisher record](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.physchem.58.032806.104637), [original article hosted by Caltech, pp. 37–41](https://www.dna.caltech.edu/courses/cs191/paperscs191/gillespie1.pdf).

The original 1977 direct-method article is [*Exact stochastic simulation of coupled chemical reactions*, DOI `10.1021/j100540a008`](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/j100540a008); its publisher endpoint returned 403 here. The detailed inspected admission is the author's 2007 article, not a claim to have inspected the 1977 full text. Erban, Chapman and Maini's *A practical guide to stochastic simulations of reaction-diffusion processes*, pinned [arXiv:0704.1908v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.1908v2), §2.1 independently introduces degradation and §2.2 distinguishes event simulation from deterministic population summaries. Their diffusion sections are outside this proposed implementation. [Original PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0704.1908v2).

## Two useful registered models

| Model | Integer state and registered events | Required explicit assumptions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pure-death` | Initial `X=N0`; `X→X-1` at propensity `kX`. `X=0` is absorbing. | Independent identical first-order losses; constant nonnegative per-item hazard; no creation, interactions, diffusion or concentration-to-count inference. |
| `two-state-pool` | Fixed total `N`, active/open count `X`; opening `X→X+1` at `alpha*(N-X)`, closing `X→X-1` at `beta*X`. Equivalent to `A⇄B`, with `A+B=N`. | Identical independent two-state entities; fixed total, constant rates; no cooperative transitions, changing environment or channel-current prediction. |

The second is a bounded birth/death **count process**, also a conservative unimolecular reaction network. It is materially different from pure loss: both boundaries can be left, trajectories fluctuate indefinitely, and two channels compete. General unbounded birth/death, bimolecular/coalescent reactions, tau-leaping, diffusion, arbitrary imported stoichiometry/propensity code and parameter fitting should wait. Count caps are resource ceilings: hitting one must stop explicitly, not reflect, clip or kill entities to create an artificial population law.

## Counts, rates and observation times

Reuse the existing time catalog/conversion helper. A first-order coefficient should use `{value,per:'s'|'ms'|'us'|'min'|'h',role:'per-item-hazard'}`, normalized by its converted time denominator. Label it `s^-1`, not cyclic `Hz`. Propensity is total event intensity in reciprocal time: `kX`, not `k` when `X>1`. Reject fractional/negative counts, unsafe integers, negative/nonfinite rates, inconsistent units and absent model assumptions before starting work. Counts are numbers of entities; molarity or moles require an explicit volume/Avogadro conversion outside this first adapter. [BIPM SI Brochure, current official edition](https://www.bipm.org/en/publications/si-brochure).

When `a0=0`, retain the absorbing state to the requested end without random draws. For positive `a0`, require finite propensities/sum, positive finite waiting time and `t+tau>t`; stop on non-advancement. If the next event exceeds the horizon, retain the current state to the horizon without firing it. Never linearly interpolate jumps. Use a declared right-continuous convention at event times and sample every replicate on the same fixed observation grid. Changing that grid must not consume PRNG draws or change the underlying event history. Do not aggregate over event timestamps: that overweights high-activity parts of the process. These are implementation policies proposed here, not an additional approximation to the reaction law.

## PRNG: replace the generated-figure teaching stream for this adapter

PP099's `mulberry32-payload-fnv1a-v1` is for deterministic generated-figure replay; its existence is not statistical-quality evidence. Recommend a small transparent **PCG-XSH-RR 64/32** implementation, ported against Melissa E. O'Neill's official minimal C reference, with exact 64-bit modular arithmetic (BigInt is the straightforward correctness-first browser option). No new npm dependency is justified merely to obtain this small algorithm. Measure its throughput under the bounded worker before promising performance.

The author's [2014 technical-report record](https://www.pcg-random.org/paper.html) provides the algorithm rationale and statistical-quality discussion. Its errata explicitly distinguishes distinct streams from stronger claims about similarity and says PCG is not intended for cryptographic use. Do not claim a theorem of replicate independence or describe a port as “audited” before review. The [official minimal implementation documentation](https://www.pcg-random.org/using-pcg-c-basic.html) documents state/sequence initialization, replay, a `2^64` period per stream and `2^63` sequence selectors. Assign the same explicit initial-state seed and a distinct bounded sequence selector per replicate, record both, and make scheduling/replicate-count changes preserve each existing replicate's stream. Avoid seed+i guesses with an undocumented hash.

Serialize full-width seeds/state/stream selectors as canonical strings, not unsafe JSON numbers or raw BigInt. Review 64-bit wrapping and the reference's 32-bit truncation/rotation carefully; JavaScript signed shifts alone are not a faithful port. Existing uint32 UI seeds can remain a deliberately smaller, exactly represented subset.

## Independent reference cases and uncertainty semantics

The following two-state-pool identities are **derived here** from independent Bernoulli entities; they are not a quoted result from the HH article. The distribution definition, mean and variance follow the [NIST binomial reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda366i.htm). Keep independent expected-value calculations outside the production propensity/PRNG/summary helpers.

| Case | Independent expectation |
| --- | --- |
| Pure death, `N0=4,k=1,t=ln(2)` | Each entity survives with probability `1/2`; `X~Binomial(4,1/2)`, probabilities `(1,4,6,4,1)/16`, mean `2`, variance `1`. With one entity this is a Bernoulli check. Gillespie (2007), equations (13)–(14), independently supports this law. |
| Closed two-state pool, `X0=0` | `p(t)=alpha/(alpha+beta)*(1-exp(-(alpha+beta)*t))`, `X~Binomial(N,p(t))` when `alpha+beta>0`. For `N=4,alpha=beta=1,t=ln(2)/2`, `p=1/4`; probabilities `(81,108,54,12,1)/256`, mean `1`, variance `3/4`. |
| Deterministic partial initial gate population | Do **not** automatically use a single binomial: `X` is the sum of independent `Binomial(X0,q11)` and `Binomial(N-X0,q01)`, where `q11=p∞+(1-p∞)exp(-ct)`, `q01=p∞*(1-exp(-ct))`, `c=alpha+beta`. Means/variances add. |
| Boundaries | Death at zero and any model with both propensities zero are absorbing. In the two-state pool, `X=0` is not absorbing when `alpha>0`, nor `X=N` when `beta>0`. Check zero-rate degeneracies, conservation, integer counts and finite horizon explicitly. |

First test deterministic mechanics with injected fixed uniforms: waiting-time inversion, categorical boundary convention, zero propensity, one event, stoichiometry, overshoot, limits and cancellation. Distributional checks should use frozen seeds, an independently computed small-state law and predeclared finite-sample allowances; report observed discrepancies rather than asserting a stochastic empirical histogram equals exact probabilities. Do not calibrate thresholds by repeatedly tuning against the same histogram. A PRNG known-answer vector is not a statistical battery; passing a battery is not biological validation.

At each shared observation time show individual trajectories, empirical mean, **sample SD across replicates**, and optionally an explicitly named empirical range/quantile band. For one replicate, SD/SE are unavailable, not zero. An optional `s/sqrt(R)` is estimated Monte Carlo error of the mean under the replicate-independence approximation; it is not the process SD, a measurement error bar or parameter uncertainty. Confidence intervals require a separately specified method and assumptions; do not call a sample spread “95% CI.” [NIST mean-confidence reference](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda352.htm). Neither exact expectation nor process variance is an ODE numerical-error estimate. Measured/parameter uncertainty is absent unless separately supplied; do not invent it.
