## Two separate questions and gates

**Domain-expert correctness review:** Does an experience preserve the source's meaning, conditions, units, provenance and limitations? A prospective expert in each relevant domain reviews the answer key and exact experience against the named primary source. Record scope of expertise, disagreements and unresolved claims. No expert has reviewed these study materials yet.

**Student/engineer usability:** Can a prospective reader find the source, distinguish reported observations from authored/model output, state an assumption and explain a parameter change without coaching? Preference ratings and task completion do not establish scientific correctness. Keep expert records and usability records separate; do not pool their scores.

A possible initial formative round is one expert and two readers per domain. This is a planning target, not a recruited sample, power calculation or promised outcome. If a domain has no available expert or readers, its gate stays pending. The 3–5-reader target in #24 is not overwritten by this plan.

## Consent, privacy and procedure

Use an anonymous participant ID and optional broad domain/familiarity categories. Do not collect names, email addresses, patient/cell-donor records, employer information or recordings by default. Explain: “We are evaluating the experience, not you. You may skip a question or stop. With your permission we will keep anonymous written observations.” Record consent before any task; declined or withdrawn participation is not a completed session.

No task requires uploading a dataset, paper, screenshot or bundle, contacting a provider, starting paid generation, sharing credentials, or transferring a participant's files. Use public/prepared material on the study device. Any optional screen sharing, recording, quotation publication or transfer requires separate explicit consent; it is not a prerequisite for participation. Remove incidental identifying information from notes before retaining or publishing them.

For each domain, alternate O→A and A→O across successive prospective readers; alternate the independently keyed A/B input forms as well. Record the actual order and prior familiarity. This is a small counterbalanced formative comparison, not a randomized effectiveness trial. Repeated exposure can create carryover: record it and do not attribute a second answer's improvement to the interface alone.

1. Obtain consent, explain the two conditions, check readability/keyboard access and record the actual environment.
2. Read the task verbatim. Ask for a prediction and reasoning **before** revealing a computed result or changing a parameter. Start timing at the explicit task start.
3. Ask the reader to locate the primary-source support, name an assumption and classify the displayed result's origin. Then permit the specified local change. Do not supply the key or steer toward it.
4. Record the answer, source locator, assistance and stop reason. Measure task time and source-location time separately; use `null` when not measured. Allow a five-minute task limit, an earlier participant stop, breaks and accessibility accommodations; record these rather than scoring speed as understanding.
5. Ask what the display establishes and what remains unsupported. Ask for the hardest moment and an optional 1–5 usefulness rating with a short explanation. The rating is not a validated learning measure.
6. Debrief any hypothetical misleading claim, confirm which anonymous notes may be retained, and offer deletion/withdrawal of the session record. A repeat after a fix needs renewed agreement and its own record.

Each reader can complete their domain's two task families in paired conditions, approximately 30–45 minutes including breaks/debrief. Experts may instead spend 45–60 minutes auditing that domain's cases. These are proposed session budgets, not observed durations.

Assess each task against its pre-reviewed key, not whether its animation looked plausible. Keep assisted answers separate from unassisted ones. A wrong reader answer alone does not prove a scientific product defect: record the exact experience/source and have an expert distinguish missing knowledge, ambiguous instructions and misleading output. Model-generated tests and user confidence do not arbitrate this decision. If two human assessors are available, preserve disagreement and its adjudication; otherwise record the single-assessor limitation.

## Revision, hold and reporting policy

A plausible scientifically misleading experience triggers a provisional hold on the affected scientific/broader-usefulness claim while the source-backed review is unresolved. Confirmed examples include presenting synthetic values as source observations, treating pseudotime as measured time/lineage, ignoring the P4 multi-interval correction, presenting an explanatory diagram as executed consensus/formal proof, mislabeling units or promoting imported check history to a fresh pass. These are **prospective criteria**, not findings from this empty study.

Retain the original source and honest read-only fallback. Correct the specific experience/label or implementation in a new revision, record the actual change, and repeat the affected task with renewed consent and an appropriate domain review. Do not declare a successful retest before it occurs. Essential inaccessible controls or broken source return also block the affected task's usability sign-off.

Only after actual observations may the owner decide a scoped formative acceptance: all confirmed critical scientific issues are fixed and rechecked; essential tasks/source access work for the recorded participants; limitations, assistance, exclusions and denominators are reported. Small convenience samples do not justify broad learning-gain, causal-effectiveness or cross-domain generalization claims, even if all observed tasks succeed.

Draft follow-up issues only from actual documented observations or separately labeled engineering evidence, with source/state/reproduction details and owner review. This protocol creates no findings or GitHub issues automatically. Until people are available, expert correctness, student usability, revision/retest and broader usefulness remain pending. #69 and #24 must not be closed on the strength of prepared materials or agent walkthroughs.
